@ HS7-MATH
1 | Sets | A set is a well-defined collection of objects called elements.
- Sets are written with braces, like A = {1, 2, 3}.
- The union of A and B contains elements in A or B; the intersection contains elements in both.
- Venn diagrams help show how sets relate to each other.
? If A = {2, 4, 6, 8} and B = {4, 8, 12}, find A ∪ B and A ∩ B.
2 | Integers and Their Operations | Integers are whole numbers and their negatives, including zero.
- Adding a negative number is the same as subtracting its positive value.
- Same signs: add and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract and use the sign of the larger absolute value.
- In multiplication and division, same signs give a positive answer and different signs give a negative answer.
? Solve: (−8) + 5, (−6) × (−3), and 20 ÷ (−4).
3 | Measurement and Conversion | Measurement uses standard units so everyone gets the same result.
- The metric system uses base units: meter (length), gram (mass), and liter (volume).
- Prefixes show size: kilo = 1,000, centi = 1/100, milli = 1/1,000.
- To convert to a smaller unit multiply; to a larger unit divide.
? Convert 2.5 kilometers to meters and 450 centimeters to meters.
4 | Algebraic Expressions | An algebraic expression combines numbers, variables, and operations.
- In 3x + 5, 3 is the coefficient, x is the variable, and 5 is the constant.
- Like terms have the same variable and exponent and can be combined.
- To evaluate an expression, substitute the value of the variable.
? Simplify 4x + 3 − 2x + 7, then evaluate it when x = 2.
5 | Linear Equations in One Variable | A linear equation in one variable can be solved by isolating the variable.
- Whatever you do to one side of the equation, do the same to the other side.
- Use inverse operations: addition undoes subtraction, multiplication undoes division.
- Check your answer by substituting it back into the equation.
? Solve 3x − 7 = 11 and check your answer.

@ HS8-MATH
1 | Factoring Polynomials | Factoring writes a polynomial as a product of simpler expressions.
- Always look first for a common monomial factor: 6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3).
- Difference of two squares: a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b).
- Trinomials like x² + 5x + 6 factor into (x + 2)(x + 3).
? Factor x² − 49 and x² + 7x + 12.
2 | Rational Algebraic Expressions | A rational algebraic expression is a fraction whose numerator and denominator are polynomials.
- Simplify by factoring and canceling common factors.
- The denominator can never equal zero.
- Add or subtract by finding a common denominator.
? Simplify (x² − 9) / (x + 3).
3 | Linear Equations in Two Variables | A linear equation in two variables, like y = 2x + 1, has a straight-line graph.
- In y = mx + b, m is the slope and b is the y-intercept.
- Slope measures steepness: rise over run.
- Any point on the line is a solution of the equation.
? Find the slope and y-intercept of y = −3x + 4, then graph it.
4 | Systems of Linear Equations | A system of linear equations is two or more equations solved together.
- The solution is the point where the lines intersect.
- Methods include graphing, substitution, and elimination.
- Parallel lines have no solution; the same line has infinitely many.
? Solve the system x + y = 10 and x − y = 2 by elimination.
5 | Triangle Congruence | Two triangles are congruent if they have the same size and shape.
- SSS: three pairs of equal sides.
- SAS: two sides and the included angle are equal.
- ASA: two angles and the included side are equal.
? Draw two triangles and explain which postulate proves they are congruent.

@ HS9-MATH
1 | Quadratic Equations | A quadratic equation has the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a is not zero.
- Solve by factoring, completing the square, or the quadratic formula.
- Quadratic formula: x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a.
- The discriminant b² − 4ac tells how many real roots there are.
? Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0 by factoring.
2 | Quadratic Functions | A quadratic function, y = ax² + bx + c, has a U-shaped graph called a parabola.
- If a is positive the parabola opens upward; if negative, downward.
- The vertex is the highest or lowest point.
- The axis of symmetry passes through the vertex.
? Find the vertex of y = x² − 4x + 3.
3 | Variation | Variation describes how one quantity changes in relation to another.
- Direct variation: y = kx, as x increases, y increases.
- Inverse variation: y = k/x, as x increases, y decreases.
- Joint variation: y = kxz, y varies with two quantities at once.
? If y varies directly with x and y = 12 when x = 3, find y when x = 7.
4 | Radicals | A radical expression contains a root, such as a square root.
- Simplify by taking out perfect squares: √50 = 5√2.
- Only like radicals can be added or subtracted.
- Rationalize the denominator to remove radicals from the bottom of a fraction.
? Simplify √72 and 3√2 + 5√2.
5 | Basic Trigonometry | Trigonometry studies the relationship between the sides and angles of right triangles.
- SOH-CAH-TOA: sine = opposite/hypotenuse, cosine = adjacent/hypotenuse, tangent = opposite/adjacent.
- The hypotenuse is the longest side, opposite the right angle.
- Trigonometry is used to find heights and distances.
? A ladder 10 m long makes a 60° angle with the ground. How high does it reach?

@ HS10-MATH
1 | Sequences | A sequence is an ordered list of numbers that follows a pattern.
- Arithmetic sequence: add the same number each time; nth term = a₁ + (n − 1)d.
- Geometric sequence: multiply by the same number each time; nth term = a₁ · rⁿ⁻¹.
- Series is the sum of the terms of a sequence.
? Find the 10th term of 3, 7, 11, 15, ...
2 | Polynomials and Polynomial Equations | A polynomial is an expression with variables raised to whole-number exponents.
- Use synthetic division to divide a polynomial by (x − c).
- The Remainder Theorem: P(c) is the remainder when P(x) is divided by (x − c).
- The Factor Theorem: (x − c) is a factor if P(c) = 0.
? Use the Remainder Theorem to find the remainder when x³ − 2x + 4 is divided by (x − 2).
3 | Circles | A circle is the set of all points equally distant from a center.
- A chord connects two points on a circle; the diameter is the longest chord.
- A central angle equals its intercepted arc.
- An inscribed angle is half its intercepted arc.
? An inscribed angle intercepts an 80° arc. What is its measure?
4 | Permutations and Combinations | Counting techniques help us find how many ways things can be arranged or chosen.
- Permutation: order matters; nPr = n! / (n − r)!.
- Combination: order does not matter; nCr = n! / [r!(n − r)!].
- Factorial: n! = n × (n − 1) × ... × 1.
? How many ways can 3 officers be chosen from 8 students for president, vice president, and secretary?
5 | Probability and Statistics | Probability measures how likely an event is, and statistics organizes and interprets data.
- Probability = favorable outcomes / total outcomes.
- Measures of position include quartiles, deciles, and percentiles.
- Data can be summarized using tables and graphs.
? What is the probability of drawing a red card from a standard deck of 52 cards?

@ HS7-SCI
1 | The Scientific Method | The scientific method is a step-by-step way of answering questions about the world.
- Steps: observe, ask a question, form a hypothesis, experiment, analyze, and conclude.
- A good experiment changes only one variable at a time.
- Results must be recorded honestly, even if they do not support the hypothesis.
? Design a simple experiment to test whether sunlight affects plant growth.
2 | Solutions | A solution is a uniform mixture of a solute dissolved in a solvent.
- In salt water, salt is the solute and water is the solvent.
- Water is called the universal solvent.
- Temperature and stirring affect how fast a solute dissolves.
? List three solutions found at home and identify the solute and solvent of each.
3 | Cells | The cell is the basic unit of life, and all living things are made of cells.
- Plant cells have a cell wall and chloroplasts; animal cells do not.
- The nucleus controls the cell's activities.
- The cell membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell.
? Draw and label a plant cell and an animal cell.
4 | Ecosystems | An ecosystem is a community of living things interacting with their nonliving environment.
- Producers make their own food; consumers eat other organisms.
- Decomposers break down dead matter and return nutrients to the soil.
- Food chains and food webs show how energy flows.
? Create a food chain with at least four organisms found in the Philippines.
5 | Motion | Motion is a change in an object's position over time.
- Distance is total path length; displacement is the straight-line change in position.
- Speed = distance ÷ time.
- Velocity includes both speed and direction.
? A jeepney travels 60 km in 1.5 hours. What is its average speed?

@ HS8-SCI
1 | Forces and Newton's Laws | Newton's laws of motion describe how forces affect the movement of objects.
- First law (inertia): an object stays at rest or in motion unless acted on by a force.
- Second law: force = mass × acceleration.
- Third law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.
? Give an everyday example of each of Newton's three laws.
2 | Work, Power, and Energy | Work is done when a force moves an object, and energy is the ability to do work.
- Work = force × distance.
- Potential energy is stored energy; kinetic energy is energy of motion.
- Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another.
? Explain the energy changes in a swinging pendulum.
3 | Earthquakes and Faults | Earthquakes happen when rocks along a fault suddenly move and release energy.
- The focus is where the earthquake starts underground; the epicenter is directly above it.
- Magnitude measures energy; intensity measures the effects on people and structures.
- The Philippines lies in the Pacific Ring of Fire, so earthquakes are common.
? Prepare an earthquake safety plan for your home.
4 | The Periodic Table | The periodic table organizes elements by atomic number and properties.
- Rows are called periods; columns are called groups or families.
- Metals are on the left, nonmetals on the right, and metalloids in between.
- Elements in the same group have similar properties.
? Identify the group and period of oxygen, sodium, and iron.
5 | The Digestive System | The digestive system breaks food down into nutrients the body can absorb.
- Digestion begins in the mouth with chewing and saliva.
- The stomach mixes food with acids and enzymes.
- Most nutrients are absorbed in the small intestine.
? Trace the path of a piece of bread through the digestive system.

@ HS9-SCI
1 | Respiratory and Circulatory Systems | These two systems work together to deliver oxygen to the body and remove carbon dioxide.
- The lungs take in oxygen and release carbon dioxide.
- The heart pumps blood through arteries, veins, and capillaries.
- Exercise and a healthy diet keep both systems strong.
? Measure your pulse before and after jumping for one minute. Explain the change.
2 | Heredity | Heredity is the passing of traits from parents to offspring through genes.
- Genes come in pairs called alleles; dominant alleles hide recessive ones.
- Gregor Mendel is known as the father of genetics.
- A Punnett square predicts the possible traits of offspring.
? Use a Punnett square to cross two parents who are both Tt for height.
3 | Biodiversity and Extinction | Biodiversity is the variety of life in an area, and it keeps ecosystems stable.
- The Philippines is one of the world's megadiverse countries.
- Habitat loss, pollution, and overharvesting threaten species.
- Endangered species include the Philippine eagle and the tamaraw.
? Suggest three actions students can take to protect local biodiversity.
4 | Volcanoes | Volcanoes form where magma rises to the Earth's surface.
- Active volcanoes have erupted recently; inactive ones have not erupted in a long time.
- Examples in the Philippines include Mayon, Taal, and Pinatubo.
- Volcanic eruptions can be dangerous but also create fertile soil.
? Explain why Mayon Volcano has a near-perfect cone shape.
5 | Chemical Bonding | Atoms join together through chemical bonds to form compounds.
- Ionic bonds form when electrons are transferred, usually between a metal and a nonmetal.
- Covalent bonds form when electrons are shared, usually between nonmetals.
- Table salt (NaCl) is ionic; water (H₂O) is covalent.
? Classify CO₂, KCl, and CH₄ as ionic or covalent.

@ HS10-SCI
1 | Plate Tectonics | The Earth's outer layer is divided into plates that move slowly over time.
- Plates can move apart (divergent), collide (convergent), or slide past each other (transform).
- Plate movement causes earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain formation.
- The Philippine Trench is formed by a convergent boundary.
? Explain how the Himalayas were formed.
2 | The Electromagnetic Spectrum | The electromagnetic spectrum is the full range of light waves, from radio waves to gamma rays.
- Order from longest to shortest wavelength: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
- Shorter wavelengths carry more energy.
- Each type has uses, such as microwaves for cooking and X-rays for medicine.
? List one use and one risk of ultraviolet radiation.
3 | The Nervous and Endocrine Systems | These systems control and coordinate the body's activities.
- The nervous system sends fast electrical signals through neurons.
- The endocrine system releases hormones into the blood for slower, longer effects.
- Both help the body maintain balance, called homeostasis.
? Explain what happens in your body when you touch something hot.
4 | DNA and Evolution | DNA carries genetic instructions, and changes over generations lead to evolution.
- DNA is a double helix made of nucleotides.
- Mutations are changes in DNA that can create new traits.
- Natural selection favors traits that help organisms survive and reproduce.
? Give an example of an adaptation that helps an animal survive in its environment.
5 | Gas Laws | Gas laws describe how pressure, volume, and temperature of gases are related.
- Boyle's Law: at constant temperature, pressure and volume are inversely related.
- Charles's Law: at constant pressure, volume and temperature are directly related.
- Temperature must be in Kelvin for gas law calculations.
? Why does a balloon shrink when placed in a refrigerator?
