- Karaniwang tauhan ang karaniwang tao, hayop, o nilalang na may kapangyarihan.

- Halimbawa sa Mindanao ang mga kuwento ng mga Maranao at Manobo.

2 | Pabula at Alamat | Ang pabula ay kuwentong hayop ang tauhan at may aral, samantalang ang alamat ay nagsasalaysay ng pinagmulan ng isang bagay o pook.
- Sa pabula, kumikilos at nagsasalita ang mga hayop na parang tao.
- Ang alamat ay sumasagot sa tanong na "Paano nagsimula ito?"
- Pareho silang nagtuturo ng mabuting asal sa mambabasa.
? Sumulat ng maikling alamat tungkol sa isang bagay sa inyong tahanan.
3 | Mga Bahagi ng Pananalita | Ang mga salita ay may kani-kaniyang gamit sa pangungusap, kaya mahalagang makilala ang bawat bahagi ng pananalita.
- Pangngalan ang tawag sa ngalan ng tao, bagay, hayop, pook, o pangyayari.
- Pandiwa ang salitang nagsasaad ng kilos o galaw.
- Pang-uri ang naglalarawan sa pangngalan, at pang-abay ang naglalarawan sa pandiwa, pang-uri, o kapwa pang-abay.
? Sumulat ng limang pangungusap at salungguhitan ang pandiwa sa bawat isa.
4 | Ibong Adarna | Ang Ibong Adarna ay korido na nagsasalaysay ng paghahanap ng tatlong prinsipe sa mahiwagang ibon na lunas sa sakit ng kanilang amang hari.
- Ang korido ay tulang may walong pantig bawat taludtod.
- Pangunahing tauhan sina Don Pedro, Don Diego, at Don Juan.
- Itinuturo nito ang kabutihang-loob, pagpapatawad, at katapatan sa pamilya.
? Ihambing ang ugali ni Don Juan sa kanyang mga kapatid.
5 | Pagsulat ng Talata | Ang talata ay pangkat ng mga pangungusap na may iisang paksa at malinaw na pagkakasunod-sunod ng ideya.
- Nagsisimula ito sa paksang pangungusap na nagsasabi ng pangunahing ideya.
- Sinusundan ito ng mga pansuportang pangungusap na nagbibigay ng detalye.
- Nagtatapos ito sa pangungusap na nagbubuod o nagbibigay-diin.
? Sumulat ng isang talata tungkol sa iyong paboritong lugar sa inyong bayan.

1 | Philippine Folk Literature | Folk literature is the oral tradition of our ancestors: myths, legends, and folk tales passed down through generations.
- Myths explain the origin of the world and natural events.
- Legends tell how a place, thing, or name came to be.
- Folk tales often teach values such as honesty and hard work.
? Retell a Philippine legend in your own words in one paragraph.
2 | Parts of Speech | Every word in a sentence has a job, and knowing these jobs helps you write clearly.
- Nouns name people, places, things, and ideas; pronouns replace nouns.
- Verbs show action or state of being.
- Adjectives describe nouns, while adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.
? Write a sentence that uses a noun, a verb, an adjective, and an adverb, then label each.
3 | Subject-Verb Agreement | A verb must agree with its subject in number: singular subjects take singular verbs and plural subjects take plural verbs.
- "She walks" is singular; "They walk" is plural.
- Subjects joined by "and" are usually plural.
- With "either...or" and "neither...nor," the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
? Correct this sentence: "The list of names are on the board."
4 | Reading for Main Idea and Details | Good readers find the main idea first, then the details that support it.
- The main idea is what the whole text is mostly about.
- Supporting details give examples, facts, or reasons.
- The main idea is often, but not always, in the first or last sentence.
? Read a short news article and write its main idea in one sentence.
5 | Writing a Personal Narrative | A personal narrative tells a true story from your own life in a clear and interesting way.
- Use the first person ("I") and tell events in order.
- Include sensory details so readers can picture the scene.
- End with what you learned or how the experience changed you.
? Write a personal narrative about a time you overcame a challenge.

1 | Afro-Asian Literature | Afro-Asian literature brings us stories, poems, and wisdom from the cultures of Africa and Asia.
- Many works reflect respect for elders, community, and tradition.
- Forms include fables, epics, haiku, and folk tales.
- Reading them helps us understand cultures different from our own.
? Compare a value found in an African folk tale with a Filipino value.
2 | Context Clues | Context clues are hints in a sentence or passage that help you figure out the meaning of an unfamiliar word.
- Definition clues explain the word directly.
- Synonym and antonym clues give a word with a similar or opposite meaning.
- Example clues show the meaning through examples.
? Find three unfamiliar words in a story and use context clues to define them.
3 | Active and Passive Voice | In the active voice the subject does the action; in the passive voice the subject receives the action.
- Active: "The students cleaned the room."
- Passive: "The room was cleaned by the students."
- Active voice is usually clearer and more direct.
? Change five passive sentences into the active voice.
4 | Analyzing Informational Texts | Informational texts give facts about a topic, and readers should judge how well the facts are organized and supported.
- Common structures include cause and effect, compare and contrast, and problem and solution.
- Text features like headings, captions, and graphs help readers.
- Check whether claims are supported by reliable evidence.
? Identify the text structure of a science article and explain your answer.
5 | Writing an Informative Essay | An informative essay explains a topic clearly using facts and examples.
- Start with an introduction that states the topic.
- Each body paragraph covers one point with supporting facts.
- End with a conclusion that sums up the key information.
? Write a short informative essay about a festival in your province.

1 | Anglo-American Literature | Anglo-American literature includes works from Britain and the United States that shaped modern English writing.
- Writers include William Shakespeare, Edgar Allan Poe, and Robert Frost.
- Many works explore themes of identity, freedom, and human nature.
- Poetry, drama, and short stories are common forms.
? Read Robert Frost's "The Road Not Taken" and explain the choice the speaker made.
2 | Elements of Drama | Drama is literature written to be performed on stage, told through dialogue and action.
- A play is divided into acts and scenes.
- Stage directions tell actors how to move and speak.
- Conflict drives the plot toward the climax and resolution.
? Write a short scene with two characters who disagree about a decision.
3 | Conditional Sentences | Conditional sentences show that one thing depends on another, often using "if."
- Zero conditional states facts: "If you heat ice, it melts."
- First conditional talks about real future possibilities: "If it rains, I will stay home."
- Second conditional imagines unlikely situations: "If I were rich, I would travel."
? Write two sentences for each type of conditional.
4 | Making Inferences | An inference is a logical conclusion based on clues in the text and your own knowledge.
- Authors do not always state everything directly.
- Look at characters' actions, words, and feelings for clues.
- Support each inference with evidence from the text.
? Read a short story and make two inferences about the main character.
5 | Writing a Literary Analysis | A literary analysis explains how an author uses elements like theme, character, or symbol to create meaning.
- State a clear thesis about the work.
- Support the thesis with quotations and explanations.
- Focus on how and why, not just what happens.
? Write a paragraph analyzing a symbol in a story you have read.

- Bias appears when a writer favors one side unfairly.

- Organize your speech with a hook, arguments, and a call to action.
- Practice eye contact, voice, and gestures.
? Prepare a two-minute speech persuading classmates to conserve water.
