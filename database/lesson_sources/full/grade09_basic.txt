> A drama, or play, is a story written to be acted out on stage.

@ HS9-MATH
# Quadratic Equations | Equations where the highest power of the variable is 2.
> When a ball is thrown or a garden's area is computed, quadratic equations often appear.
= Quadratic equation :: an equation in the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0.
= Roots :: the values of x that make the equation true.
= Factoring method :: solving by writing the equation as a product equal to zero.
= Quadratic formula :: x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a.
! Example :: Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0. Factor: (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0. So x = 2 or x = 3.
? Is x² + 3x − 4 = 0 a quadratic equation? :: Yes
? Solve x² − 9 = 0. :: x = 3 or x = −3
? In 2x² + 3x − 5 = 0, what are a, b, and c? :: a = 2, b = 3, c = −5

# Quadratic Functions | Functions whose graph is a parabola.
> The path of a basketball shot forms a curve called a parabola, which is the graph of a quadratic function.
= Quadratic function :: a function in the form y = ax² + bx + c, where a ≠ 0.
= Parabola :: the U-shaped graph of a quadratic function.
= Vertex :: the highest or lowest point of the parabola.
= Axis of symmetry :: the vertical line through the vertex that divides the parabola into two equal halves.
* If a is positive, the parabola opens upward. If a is negative, it opens downward.
! Example :: y = x² opens upward and its vertex is (0, 0). y = −x² + 4 opens downward and its vertex is (0, 4).
? What is the graph of a quadratic function called? :: Parabola
? Does y = −2x² open upward or downward? :: Downward
? What is the vertex of y = x² + 3? :: (0, 3)

@ HS9-SCI
# Respiratory and Circulatory Systems | How the body takes in oxygen and moves blood.
> Every cell in your body needs oxygen. Two body systems work together to deliver it.
= Respiratory system :: takes in oxygen and releases carbon dioxide; includes the nose, trachea, and lungs.
= Circulatory system :: moves blood around the body; includes the heart, blood, and blood vessels.
= Alveoli :: tiny air sacs in the lungs where oxygen enters the blood.
= Arteries and veins :: arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry blood back to the heart.
! Example :: When you run, you breathe faster to take in more oxygen, and your heart beats faster to deliver it to your muscles.
? Where does oxygen enter the blood? :: In the alveoli of the lungs
? What do arteries do? :: Carry blood away from the heart.
? Why does your heart beat faster when you exercise? :: To deliver more oxygen to the muscles.

# Heredity | How traits are passed from parents to children.
> You may have your mother's eyes or your father's height. These traits are passed on through heredity.
= Heredity :: the passing of traits from parents to offspring.
= Gene :: a part of DNA that carries a trait.
= Dominant trait :: a trait that shows even if only one parent passes it.
= Recessive trait :: a trait that shows only if both parents pass it.
= Gregor Mendel :: known as the "Father of Genetics" for his experiments with pea plants.
! Example :: T = tall (dominant), t = short (recessive). A plant with Tt is tall. Only a plant with tt is short.
? What is heredity? :: The passing of traits from parents to offspring.
? Who is the Father of Genetics? :: Gregor Mendel
? Is a plant with Tt tall or short? :: Tall

# Occupational Health and Safety | Protecting workers from hazards at work.

= Occupational health and safety (OHS) :: practices that protect workers' health and safety.

= Ergonomic hazard :: poor posture or repetitive movements that strain the body.
! Example :: A computer technician uses an anti-static wrist strap and sits with proper posture to avoid electrical and ergonomic hazards.
? What is OHS? :: Practices that protect workers' health and safety.
